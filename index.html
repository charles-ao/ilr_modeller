Announced policy, not yet law

Earned settlement was announced in the government's consultation response . It is not yet in the Immigration Rules, and the final rules may change. This tool is illustrative and is not immigration advice. Before you make decisions, speak to an OISC-regulated adviser or an immigration solicitor.

      How we count

      Each route needs a minimum time on your route and enough years at or above an earnings threshold. For example, the 5-year route needs 3 of the 5 years before you apply to reach £50,271. The government hasn't said what a “year” means. This tool counts it three ways and shows them side by side.

      BasisA “year” isYears counted before applying
      Tax year6 April to 5 AprilThe most recent complete tax years ending before the application date
      Calendar year1 January to 31 DecemberThe most recent complete calendar years ending before the application date
      Rolling 12 monthsAny 12 consecutive monthsBack-to-back 12-month blocks, counted back from the day before the application date

      The calculation

      • Each month earns one twelfth of the annual salary in force that month. Months before your route start earn £0.
      • An application date is the first day of a month. For each route and way of counting, the tool steps forward month by month from the earliest point you could have enough time on route. It stops at the first month that also passes the earnings test.
      • On the tax-year basis, pay is allocated April to March, and dates are shown as 6 April.
      • Years that haven't finished by the application date are ignored.
      • Results run to years after your route start. A route not met by then shows “Not reached”.
      • If a later pay cut means the test stops being met, the tool shows the date eligibility lapses.

      What this tool doesn't check

      The tool models only the earnings test and time on route. Under the announced rules, you'd also need to meet every one of these:

      • English at B2 (higher than today's B1), unless you're exempt.
      • Life in the UK test passed.
      • No criminal record as an adult. This includes cautions, fines and community sentences.
      • No outstanding government debt, including NHS charges, unpaid tax and National Insurance.
      • No public funds in the 5 years before applying. Otherwise you're on the 20-year route.
      • No immigration breaches as an adult, such as overstaying or breaching visa conditions. Otherwise you're on the 20-year route.
      • An eligible route. Only Skilled Worker, Global Talent, Scale-up, Innovator Founder and International Sportsperson can use the 3 or 5-year earnings routes.
      • Unbroken residence and the absence limits for your route.

      Some people can reach 5 years in other ways, which this tool doesn't model:

      • Eligible health and education jobs on national pay scales.
      • Partners, parents and children of British citizens.
      • UK Ancestry.
      • Transitional arrangements for care workers and some public-service workers who were on their route before May 2025.

      Couples can combine earnings against a household threshold. That isn't modelled either.
